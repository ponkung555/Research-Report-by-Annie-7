# Editorial Review — The Salt Battery — How Sodium-ion Is Rewriting the EV Price Floor
*Reviewer: Enough (Chief Editor, Neverland) — 2026-05-10*

## Lede
It landed. "Under the floor sits a CATL Naxtra cell pack" — specific, physical, grounded before the chemistry begins. "The eleventh element is not scarce" closes the paragraph with the argument. This is the standard.

## What Held
- Lede opens on a specific object under a specific car, then backs out through the chemistry to the periodic table. Physical, concrete, argument-in-three-words. Cleared.
- Close is the strongest since Report 5: "Not because sodium-ion is better than lithium — it isn't, on the metrics lithium leads. Because it is cheaper, colder, and available in every country on earth." Counterintuitive, outward-pointing, earned.
- Convergence moves land correctly throughout — Resource Sovereignty (two problems, one elemental property), the hybrid pack (two range problems, one architecture), 0V discharge (two commercial problems, one electrochemical characteristic). The lesson from the last two reviews applied consistently across multiple sections for the first time.

## What to Sharpen
1. **Prussian Blue Analog lacks a physical image.** "Iron-based, abundant, processable at lower temperatures" is a glossary entry. The material has its physical identity in its name — "Prussian Blue" is one of the oldest industrial pigments, a vivid blue-green used in paint for three centuries before anyone thought to put it in a battery. The piece strips the color out and treats the name as a chemistry label. Lesson 1 applies beyond hardware: when a material has a color, texture, or scale, that is the anchor. Use it before the chemistry.

2. **"Hard Carbon" section closes with a mop-up sentence.** "The supply chain for hard carbon anodes and Prussian Blue Analog cathodes is still building out; 2026 is the breakout year for sodium-ion in commercial vehicles, with supply chain infrastructure still maturing around the chemistry." This says the same thing twice — building out, still maturing — and ends the section on a hedge rather than a conclusion. The header makes a claim; the close should confirm or deepen it, not qualify it and move on.

3. **Headline scope and article scope diverge.** The title promises an EV price floor story; the piece covers grid storage, freight shipping, and transit fleets as primary sodium-ion markets — distinct applications, distinct economics. Either the headline widens to match what the piece actually argues, or the grid and freight sections need explicit bridges back to the passenger EV frame. Angle discipline: one lens, P1 to close.

## The One Thing
Physical images for unfamiliar materials are still being dropped when the chemistry feels technical. "Prussian Blue" has its color in the name — the piece treats it as a label and loses the image entirely. Lesson 1 is not only for hardware sections. Anywhere a reader encounters a material they cannot picture, find the one physical detail that makes it real before the chemistry arrives.
